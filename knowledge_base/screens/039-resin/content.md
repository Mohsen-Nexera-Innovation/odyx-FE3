## 1. Who is reading, and what do they want

| | |
|---|---|
| Primary audience | Dentists + dental labs |
| Where they are in the journey | Comparing — they own or are evaluating a printer and are deciding whether the materials layer is trustworthy. Resin is also a search entry point ("surgical guide resin"), so some arrive cold, mid-chain |
| What they're actually asking | "Which resin do I need for this job?" · "Is it certified — can I legally and safely put it in a mouth?" · "What are the wash & cure settings?" · "Where's the data sheet?" |
| What makes them leave | Range-level claims with no documents behind them; a wall of identical bottles with no way to pick a lane; specs with no test conditions; no Arabic |

**Dentist vs lab:** the **dentist** is sold by indications-first cards, natural shades, and the
chairside argument (a temporary printed, cured in ~10 minutes, and seated the same day). The
**lab** is sold by repeatability and safety-of-handling: published cure settings per application,
wash-on-plate keeping hands away from uncured resin, batch-to-batch consistency, MSDS on file.
Sections 3 and 5 speak dentist; sections 4, 6 and 7 carry the lab register.

### Section 4 · Which resin for which job
- **Eyebrow:** COMPARE
- **Headline:** Which resin for which job?
- **Sub:** Every property explained in clinical terms — tap any term to see why it matters.
- **Matrix rows → line:** Permanent crowns, veneers, inlays/onlays → Ceramic Crown, Crown & Bridge ·
  Bridges & denture teeth → Crown & Bridge · Temporary crowns & bridges → Temporary Restoration
  (Ceramic Crown also covers temporary restorations — child pages clarify when the client answers
  Q8) · Study / working models → Ortho Model 2.0 · Aligner & thermoforming models → Ortho Model 2.0 ·
  Implant surgical guides → Surgical Guide Pro
- **Glossary tooltips (the teaching layer, verbatim-close to catalog):**
  - *Flexural strength* — resistance to bending under chewing load; 140–160 MPa reads as "very
    strong — essential for permanent restorations."
  - *Heat deflection temperature* — above 100 °C a part withstands sterilization; around 60 °C
    suits models only.
  - *Elongation at break* — 5–10 % behaves ceramic-like and brittle; 100 %+ behaves elastic,
    gingiva-like.
  - *Water absorption* — lower is better: dimensional stability, no staining, no odor.
  - *Shore D hardness* — surface hardness of rigid materials (Shore A is for soft, gingiva-like ones).
- **Microcopy:** Full per-line technical data arrives with each line's data sheet (→ #downloads).

### Section 6 · Wash & cure settings
- **Eyebrow:** AFTER THE PRINT
- **Headline:** Printed isn't finished.
- **Body:** A printed part is washed in IPA, then post-cured — that's what turns a print into a
  restoration. ODYX publishes the settings instead of leaving your lab to guess: standard models
  cure in about 2 minutes, surgical guides in about 3, temporary crowns in about 10 (settings may
  vary by resin type). One ODYX Cure box covers the whole range, because its three wavelengths —
  365, 385 and 405 nm — are selectable per resin. For labs, the UW-03 washes models while they're
  still on the build plate, keeping hands away from uncured resin.
- **In-line link:** See the curing machines → 037
- **Microcopy:** Biocompatibility depends on the resin — which is why these claims live here, per line, and not on the cure page.

## 8. Arabic
Transcreation, not translation.

- Terminology: resin = **راتنج**, plural **راتنجات** (page title: راتنجات ODYX). Curing = **المعالجة الضوئية**
  (light-curing; more precise than تصليب). Wash & cure = **الغسل والمعالجة**. Indication = **استطباب**
  (the clinical register dentists use). Same-day = **في نفس الجلسة** ("in the same visit" — truer
  to chairside reality than حرفيًا "same day"). Line/model names and shade codes stay Latin.
- Numerals: **Western (0–9)** for all specs, shades and timings — sitewide decision per MASTER §3.
- Headline that won't survive literal translation: "Engineered for Precision. Built for Every
  Indication." → proposed transcreation: **«دقة في كل تفصيلة. راتنج لكل استطباب.»** ("precision in
  every detail; a resin for every indication") — keeps the two-beat structure without the
  passive-voice awkwardness of a literal render.
- "Printed isn't finished." → **«الطباعة ليست النهاية.»**
- Length check: Arabic runs slightly shorter but taller (line-height 1.8) — the risk is the trust
  chips and the matrix headers, both of which must not truncate; eyebrow style switches to
  weight-700 + accent (no uppercase exists).

**Take**
- **Formlabs' per-line child skeleton** — it independently confirms the client's own per-line references almost section-for-section (hero → chips → description → why → applications → trust → spec table → post-processing → CTA). Take the *order and depth*, especially the **spec table + test-conditions footnote** and **Post-Processing as a first-class section** — that section is our built-in cross-sell to ODYX Cure / UW-03, and we actually hold real per-application cure times (p15).
- **Indications-first blocks** ("*ideal for:*" before any chemistry) — Formlabs. A clinician picks material by job, not by MPa.
- **SprintRay's one-line clinical "why" per metric** — the exact mechanism `products.md` p24–p26 already gives us as the properties glossary. Ours can teach honestly instead of comparing against ghosts.
- **Formlabs' document architecture** — client asked for "data sheets and safety documents" verbatim; a tabbed TDS/SDS block with per-line artifacts is the systematized answer. (Artifacts themselves are blocked on the Scientific Team.)
- **Trust placed early** — but restated **per line, never per range**: two of five lines carry no CE/FDA (p30). Formlabs badges its facility; we badge each line honestly.
- **Vocabulary**: "indications", "ideal for", "biocompatible", shade codes (A1–B2), "wash & cure settings", "instructions for use", "technical data sheet".

**Reject**
- SprintRay's **vs-competition bar charts** — we have no comparative test data; every unsourced number is a credibility loss (claims rule).
- SprintRay's **17-block undifferentiated stack** — that is complaint 2 ("every screen looks the same") applied to one page.
- Formlabs' **commerce-first spine** (price in hero, bulk ladders, cart) — pricing policy is unanswered (products.md Q7); ODYX sells through demos and distributors.
- Stratasys' **faceted catalog** — five SKUs don't need facets, and zero narrative is complaint 6.
- **Range-level regulatory badges** of any kind — false for two of five lines.

**Where we beat all four.** Nobody puts the workflow on the resin page. SprintRay claims "scan to delivery" in prose with no steps or parameters; Formlabs shows real wash/cure settings but hides scan/design behind an application-guide link; Stratasys treats material as SKU metadata; 3Shape has no materials at all. A resin page that renders **SCAN → DESIGN → PRINT → WASH & CURE → DELIVER as an on-page module, with this material's own validated parameters at each step** (cure times per application, p15) is unoccupied ground — and it is precisely the connected-workflow concept the client is buying. Second gap: **no competitor compares across their own line.** An honest five-line comparison built on the teaching glossary (p24–p26) answers "which resin for which job?" — the first question a dentist asks — and no benchmark does it.
